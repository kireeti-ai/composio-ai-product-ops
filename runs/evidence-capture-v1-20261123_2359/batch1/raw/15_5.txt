How limits apply Per organization. All API tokens in your organization share the same allowance. Creating more tokens does not raise it. Per endpoint. Each endpoint has its own allowance. For example, requests to GET /issues do not count against POST /issues/search . Per minute. Every endpoint uses one of three limits: 30, 120, or 300 requests per minute, unless Pylon has raised the limit for your organization. X-Rate-Limit-Limit shows the limit that applies to you. Each endpoint's limit is listed in its entry in the API reference . If your integration needs a higher limit on a specific endpoint, contact Pylon support. Response headers Header When Value X-Rate-Limit-Limit Every response Your organization's per-minute allowance for this endpoint. X-Rate-Limit-Remaining Every response How many more requests you can make to this endpoint right now. X-Rate-Limit-Reset Every response Seconds until your full per-minute allowance is restored. Retry-After 429 Too Many Requests only Seconds to wait before the next request can succeed, rounded up to a whole second. X-Retry-After 429 Too Many Requests only Seconds until your full per-minute allowance is restored. Capacity comes back gradually before then, so some requests can succeed sooner. A request over the limit returns 429 with the rate_limited error code. See Errors . Handling 429 responses Watch X-Rate-Limit-Remaining and slow down as it nears zero, rather than waiting for a 429 . Compare it with X-Rate-Limit-Limit and X-Rate-Limit-Reset to pace bulk jobs before you hit the limit. When you get a 429 , wait at least the number of seconds in Retry-After before retrying. Add backoff and jitter if you keep receiving 429 responses. X-Retry-After describes the longer wait until the full allowance is restored. Spread bulk jobs over time instead of sending them in bursts. Remember that every integration using your organization's tokens draws from the same allowance. Prefer search endpoints with filters and pagination over fetching objects one at a time.
