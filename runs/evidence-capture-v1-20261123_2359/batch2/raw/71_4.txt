Choose a personal access token, internal connection, OAuth integration, or public integration based on who will use your app and how it needs to access Notion.
Choose the right option
Automate your own work
Use a personal access token for scripts, tools, and Workers that run with your Notion permissions.
Build a shared workspace tool
Use an internal connection for tools your team shares, with access granted to the connection independently of any one person.
Build an app for your workspaces
Use an OAuth integration for internal tools where each person authorizes their own access. Your app acts on behalf of the user who connects it.
Build an app for any workspace
Use a public integration so users in any workspace can authorize your app through OAuth. You can also apply to list it on the Marketplace.
Compare the options
OAuth integrations and public integrations use the same OAuth authorization flow. Here, 
OAuth integrations
 are limited to selected workspaces, while 
public integrations
 are available to any workspace.

Shared concepts
Capabilities
Capabilities
 determine which operations a connection or token can perform, such as reading pages, updating databases, or adding comments. Choose the capabilities your app needs.
Content access
Capabilities and content access work together: permission to update pages does not grant access to every page. A personal access token uses your existing permissions. An internal connection needs content access granted to it. For OAuth integrations and public integrations, each user grants access during authorization. To share content:
Internal connections:
 Add the page or database in the Developer portal’s 
Content access
 tab, or open it in Notion and use 
Add connections
. See 
Granting page access
 for the steps.
OAuth integrations and public integrations:
 Each user selects pages in the OAuth page picker when authorizing the app. Selecting a parent page also grants access to its child pages. See 
Selecting pages during authorization
.
Personal access tokens:
 No separate sharing step is needed. The token uses its creator’s existing permissions, limited by its capabilities.
Connection owners
Connection owners can change a connection’s settings, refresh its credentials, and delete it. Internal connections, OAuth integrations, and public integrations can have more than one owner. See 
Share a connection
 to add people or groups.
Webhooks
Internal connections, OAuth integrations, and public integrations can subscribe to 
webhooks
 to respond to changes such as page updates and new comments. Use these when your app needs to react to changes without repeatedly checking the API.
Getting started
Try the 
Quickstart
 to create a page with a personal access token. For setup instructions and permissions, choose one of the guides above.
See 
Authorization
 for token authentication and OAuth flows, and 
API credential security
 for storing and protecting credentials.
